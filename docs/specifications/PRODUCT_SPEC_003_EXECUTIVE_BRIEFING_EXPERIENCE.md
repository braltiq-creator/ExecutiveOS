# PRODUCT_SPEC_003 — Executive Briefing Experience

**Status:** Formal product specification (experience)  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Product surface:** Executive Briefing (Today)  
**Authority:** Bound by Constitution · Narrative v1.1 · Product Strategy · Product Principles · Core Executive Loop · PRODUCT_SPEC_001 · PRODUCT_SPEC_002 · Executive Experience Blueprint · Product Development Lifecycle · ADRs 001–006  

**Nature:** Defines what the Executive Briefing *feels like to use* — not UI, layouts, wireframes, technical architecture, or engineering.

PRODUCT_SPEC_001 defines what the Briefing *is*.  
PRODUCT_SPEC_002 defines how reasoning *behaves*.  
This specification defines how a human leader *lives through* the Briefing.

Every future UX decision must preserve the experience obligations here. Screens may change. The felt contract must not.

---

## 1. Experience Vision

### The ideal Executive Briefing experience

The ideal experience is the opposite of a dashboard morning.

The executive does not arrive to explore modules, configure widgets, or chat with software. They arrive to **boot judgement** — the way one boots an operating system before doing real work. Within a few calm minutes, the world of overnight noise has been reduced to a short, ranked understanding of what requires leadership attention, why it matters to what they are trying to achieve, and what they should do next.

The Briefing feels like software designed around the principles of an exceptional Chief of Staff: the reading has been done; the mandate is held; the consequential few calls are ready; authority remains with the leader.

It does not feel like a human colleague. It does not perform warmth, sparkle, or personality. It performs **Clarity** — and through clarity, **Confidence**.

### What the executive should feel

| Feeling | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Recognised** | “This knows what I’m trying to achieve.” |
| **Oriented** | “I know where the centre of the day is.” |
| **Unburdened** | “I am not responsible for scanning everything.” |
| **Capable** | “I understand my business better than five minutes ago.” |
| **Calm** | “Urgency, if present, is earned — not theatrical.” |
| **In authority** | “I decide. The system proposes.” |
| **Willing to return** | “Tomorrow’s first minutes belong here.” |

They should not feel entertained, hustled, scored, or managed. They should not feel that software is competing with them for control of the narrative.

### What they should accomplish

In a normal morning use:

1. Confirm or re-absorb Strategic Intent as the lens for the day  
2. Identify the lead judgement (or honestly register that none is required)  
3. Understand stakes on Focus Outcomes without reconstructing five systems  
4. Know the next move — decide, protect focus, unblock, prepare for a binding conversation, or wait  
5. Leave with Confidence Through Clarity — what matters, what can wait, and why the ignored rest can stay ignored  

Success is not “finished reading everything.” Success is **judgement readiness**.

Narrative v1.1’s success definition applies directly: we succeed when an executive can form their first important decision of the day without first opening a scattered set of systems to reconstruct what matters. The Briefing experience is that reconstruction — already done, already ranked, still challengeable.

### Experience as category proof

If the Briefing experience feels like a dashboard, ExecutiveOS becomes a dashboard company — whatever the Narrative says.  
If it feels like a chatbot, ExecutiveOS becomes a chatbot company.  
If it feels like calm ranked judgement support, ExecutiveOS becomes a Decision Operating System in practice.

PRODUCT_SPEC_003 exists because category is not only declared. Category is *experienced* every morning. This specification is the experiential proof obligation for DecisionOS.

## 2. First Launch Experience

First launch is not onboarding theatre. It is the first chance to earn the morning habit — or to permanently train the executive to treat ExecutiveOS as optional software.

### Emotional experience

The executive arrives time-poor and appropriately sceptical. They have been promised “visibility” before. They have been asked for broad permissions before. They have opened empty dashboards before.

The first emotional obligation is safety:

> This will not waste my attention, demand a project to configure, or pretend certainty it does not have.

Only then can curiosity open. Only then can the magic moment occur.

### How the product introduces itself

Introduction is institutional and brief — Decision Operating System energy, not consumer splash or AI carnival.

The product introduces itself by **doing the job early**:

- Identity is recognised (organisation context, not a personality quiz)  
- A minimal trust step unlocks observation (connect what improves judgement context — typically the collaboration suite first)  
- Strategic Intent appears as a draft to confirm, labelled as inferred where appropriate  
- The Briefing appears — even partial — with honest gaps rather than a locked gate behind “complete setup”  

The introduction is: *We prepare judgement. You decide. Here is what we already understand. Correct us. Then see today.*

It is not: *Welcome to your AI-powered command centre. Complete these twelve steps. Meet your agents. Tour the modules.*

### How trust is established

Trust on first launch is Progressive Trust in miniature:

| Move | Trust effect |
|------|----------------|
| Ask only for scopes that improve Observe/Understand | Least privilege feels adult |
| Explain the ask in one plain sentence | Access is not extractive mystery |
| Show a drafted Intent that can be edited | Recognition without lock-in |
| Surface a Briefing with named gaps | Honesty over fabrication |
| Avoid payment / team / deep CRM pressure before value | Value before commitment |
| Make correction easy when inference is wrong | Partnership, not bluffing |

Trust is not a paragraph titled “Our commitment to security.” Trust is the felt sequence: small ask → visible value → easy correction → invitation to go deeper later.

If first launch fails emotionally — confusion, guilt, empty spectacle, or interrogation — later excellence rarely recovers the habit.

### First launch anti-goals (experiential)

Do not make the executive feel:

- That they purchased unfinished software  
- That IT homework precedes leadership value  
- That an AI persona is “onboarding them”  
- That declining a connector means a dead product  
- That the company wants their data more than their clarity  

Do make them feel:

- That someone (or something disciplined) has already tried to understand their mandate  
- That honesty about gaps is part of the craft  
- That the first consequential judgement appears early  
- That leaving and returning tomorrow is natural  

First launch is where Narrative’s “discipline to say no” meets the human. Saying no to spectacle is how trust starts.

### The first-launch magic moment

The Experience Blueprint’s magic moment remains the north star:

Recognised + Ranked + Explained + Actionable — ideally within the first session, even with thin data.

A partial Briefing that says “pipeline signals limited until Salesforce is connected” plus one real judgement call is success. A polished shell with nothing consequential is failure.

## 3. Daily Morning Ritual

The ideal morning is a ritual of clarity, not a ritual of consumption.

Target emotional arc: **arrival load → orientation → recognition → focus → readiness → calm exit.**

Typical duration for a habitual user: on the order of **a few minutes**, not a reading session. Depth is optional and intentional — not the default tax.

### Minute 0 — Arrival

**What happens**  
They open Today. The Decision Operating System boots. There is no demand to choose a destination among peers. The Briefing *is* the destination.

**Emotional change**  
From ambient overload (inbox, calendar anxiety, unfinished threads) toward a single centre. The body of attention narrows. Something like relief begins if prior mornings have earned it; on early days, wary hope.

**What must not happen**  
A modal tour. A chatbot greeting. A wall of equal cards. A notification badge war.

### Minute 1 — Orientation and recognition

**What happens**  
Strategic Intent is present — why we are focusing here this horizon. The mandate is felt before the noise. Outcome Health is available as stakes, not as a game score. The lead judgement line begins to form: what requires attention.

**Emotional change**  
From “where do I start?” to “I know the centre.” Recognition: *this is my business, my focus, my period.* If Intent is wrong, correction is the work of this minute — not later, not buried.

**What must not happen**  
Making them hunt for why the day is framed as it is. Hiding Non-Focus noise as if it were equal priority.

### Minute 3 — Understanding the stakes

**What happens**  
Overnight or material changes that affect Focus (and critical Watching) are already interpreted. Priority Decisions appear as questions with consequence. Why precedes any prescription. Uncertainty is named where it exists.

**Emotional change**  
From recognition to evaluation. The executive is thinking, not scavenging. Cognitive load falls because ranking has already happened. Confidence begins if explanations are challengeable and grounded in Outcomes.

**What must not happen**  
A flood of “insights.” Charts before questions. Recommendations without recognition. False urgency tone.

### Minute 5 — Readiness

**What happens**  
The executive knows the next move: open a Decision, protect focus, unblock an Action path, prepare for a binding conversation, confirm nothing urgent, or go deeper on one Outcome. Calendar context, if needed, orients those conversations without becoming the hero.

**Emotional change**  
From evaluation to **judgement readiness**. Authority feels intact. The ignored rest feels legitimately ignored. They can close — or deepen — without guilt.

**What must not happen**  
A sense that finishing requires reading everything. A cliff of unfinished setup. A push to “keep exploring.”

### After the ritual

The rest of the morning belongs to leadership work — meetings, decisions, people — not to living inside ExecutiveOS. The Briefing’s job was to boot clarity. If they return midday, it should be for a specific deepening, not because the product is needy.

### Ritual variations (same contract)

Not every morning is identical; the contract is.

- **Travel morning:** even shorter path to lead judgement; Board Mode if presenting remotely  
- **Post-board morning:** continuity from yesterday’s decisions; less re-litigation; more execution/unblock posture  
- **Crisis morning:** still sparse — weight without volume; false urgency more dangerous than ever  
- **First week mornings:** more Intent confirmation; more named gaps; same refusal of setup theatre  

The ritual may flex. It may not become a different product.

### Midday and evening (secondary to morning)

The defining ritual is morning. Midday return should feel like reopening a known centre for one deepening — not like a second full boot demanding re-consumption. Evening, if present at all, should favour closure and carry-forward — never guilt, streaks, or “you missed three insights.”

Experience Blueprint rhythms apply: ExecutiveOS is not a place to live all day. The Briefing experience succeeds when it liberates the day, not colonises it.

### Habit formation across the first week

Day one aims for the magic moment.  
Day two aims for recognition without re-teaching.  
Day three aims for trust in suppression (“I didn’t check the other five tools first”).  
Day five aims for ritual inevitability: opening Today is simply how the day starts.

If by day five the executive still treats the Briefing as optional content, the experience has not yet become infrastructure — investigate false urgency, thin recognition, or setup friction, not “engagement campaigns.”

## 4. Executive Attention Journey

Attention is the scarce resource. The Briefing experience is an attention journey from wide world → narrow centre → optional depth → release.

### What is seen first

1. **Centre of mandate** — Strategic Intent (the lens)  
2. **Lead claim on judgement** — the single most valuable thing to know or judge now  
3. **Stakes anchor** — Outcome Health as portfolio truth  
4. **Material change** — what moved overnight that affects Focus  

First attention is sacred. Whatever occupies it teaches the executive what ExecutiveOS believes “important” means. If first attention is a promotional tip, a chat prompt, or a secondary module, the product has lied about its philosophy.

### What is ignored (by design)

The experience must make ignoring feel safe:

- Non-Focus themes suppressed unless exceptional consequence forces a breach (with explanation)  
- Operational storms that do not require executive judgement  
- Recency without strategic relevance  
- Equal departmental urgencies  
- Depth that belongs behind progressive disclosure  

Ignored content is not missing content. It is **ranked out**. The emotional gift is permission not to look.

### What is progressively revealed

```
Glance     Intent + lead judgement + health signal
Scan       Overnight change + priority decisions (sparse)
Read       Insights / actions / calendar only as needed
Deep       Decision, Outcome, Intent detail — leaving the Briefing’s calm centre
```

Progressive disclosure is not “more widgets below the fold.” It is a disciplined widening only when the executive chooses depth. The Briefing never ambushes with the Knowledge graph, admin, or advisor theatre as the price of morning clarity.

### How attention moves

Healthy attention path:

**Lens → Lead → Stakes → Change → Questions → Optional recommend → Optional context → Exit or one deep path.**

Unhealthy attention path:

**Widget → Widget → Chat → Chart → Settings → Exhaustion.**

PRODUCT_SPEC_002’s priority model exists so this journey can stay healthy. PRODUCT_SPEC_003 insists the *felt* journey matches that model: sparse survivors, explained, human-ending.

### Attention and guilt

A subtle failure mode is **attention guilt**: the executive finishes the Briefing but feels they “should” keep scanning other systems to be sure.

The experience must actively dissolve that guilt when ranking is sound — through explainability (“why this can wait”), Intent boundaries (Non-Focus is deliberate), and consistency when they spot-check Outcome detail.

If spot-checks repeatedly reveal buried material consequence, the engine failed — and the experience must not gaslight. If spot-checks confirm suppression was correct, trust deepens and guilt fades. That loop is how mornings get shorter over time.

## 5. Executive Confidence Journey

Confidence Through Clarity is the experiential outcome. Confidence is not bravado. It is the settled sense that the picture is clear enough to judge — including clarity about what is unknown.

### How confidence grows

| Moment | Confidence effect |
|--------|-------------------|
| Intent matches reality | “I am not fighting the frame.” |
| Lead story is consequential and explained | “I know the centre.” |
| Outcomes ground stakes | “This is about results, not vibes.” |
| Gaps are named | “I am not being sold false completeness.” |
| Recommendations are rejectable | “I remain the decider.” |
| Corrections stick | “The system learns without sulking.” |
| Quiet days are honest | “Silence can be trusted.” |
| Tomorrow repeats the gift | “This is infrastructure.” |

Confidence compounds across mornings. A single clever session does not create habit; a week of accurate, calm boots does.

### Where uncertainty is explained

Uncertainty appears as part of the story, not as fine print:

- Inferred vs confirmed Intent or signals  
- Missing connectors that would change commercial or calendar confidence  
- Conflicting sources presented as the judgement, not averaged away  
- Low confidence attached to any recommendation that still deserves airtime  

Uncertainty explained increases confidence. Uncertainty hidden destroys it when reality disagrees later.

### How trust increases

Trust increases when small moments stay true:

- No cry-wolf  
- No fabrication  
- No authority seizure  
- No surprise permission grabs  
- No contradiction between Briefing stakes and Outcome detail  

Trust decreases when the experience feels busy, needy, or performative — even if “features” are rich.

The confidence journey ends each session with a simple internal sentence the product should earn:

> I know what matters. I know what can wait. I can face the next call.

### Confidence versus comfort

Comfort can be false: soothing copy, green indicators, “you’re all caught up” when judgement is needed.  
Confidence is true: clear stakes, clear gaps, clear next move — including the move to wait.

The experience must prefer true confidence over false comfort. Uncertain days that name uncertainty produce more Confidence Through Clarity than busy days that hide it.

### Confidence and other people

When the executive carries Briefing clarity into a 1:1, ELT, or board setting, confidence should transfer: the same centre, the same stakes language, the same Outcome grounding. If the Briefing only works in private and collapses in social leadership contexts, Board Mode and consistency with Outcome/Decision detail have failed the confidence journey.

## 6. AI Behaviour During Experience

AI is present as an advisor inside the experience — never as the host of the experience.

### When AI speaks

- To explain why something ranked  
- To summarise overnight change without stripping stakes  
- To attach Outcome/Intent grounding in plain language  
- To draft clarity when deepening into a decision narrative (secondary to core Briefing calm)  

Speech is sparse, institutional, and challengeable.

### When AI stays silent

- When structured ranking already carries the morning  
- When the day is honestly quiet  
- When confidence is too low to add value beyond a named gap  
- When speaking would only fill space  
- When advisor capabilities are unavailable — the Briefing still boots on structured truth  

Silence is competence.

### When AI asks

Rarely. Only when stakes are high and confidence is low — preferably as confirm/edit of a draft (Intent, Focus boundary), not as an interrogation.

Questions during the Briefing experience must feel like respect for judgement, not like a ticket form.

### When AI recommends

Only after recognition and explanation. Only on sparse survivors. Always as a proposal.

Recommendations during the experience should feel like a prepared option from a disciplined chief-of-staff craft — never like an autopilot.

### When AI simply observes

Most of the time. Observation is continuous and largely invisible. The executive experiences the *result* of observation (ranked understanding), not a livestream of the engine thinking.

Performing “thinking” as spectacle violates Calm Over Noise and Confidence Through Restraint.

### AI presence summary

| Mode | Felt frequency | Felt quality |
|------|----------------|--------------|
| Observe | Constant, invisible | Competence without performance |
| Explain | Common on survivors | Grounding, challengeable |
| Recommend | Rare, sparse | Proposal, not command |
| Ask | Rare | High stakes, low confidence |
| Silent | Often | Trustworthy quiet |

If AI presence ever becomes the star of the morning, the experience has inverted: DecisionOS became an AI show. Restore the Briefing as the star; return AI to advisor craft.

## 7. Information Consumption Principles

### How much information?

As little as required for judgement readiness — then stop.

Governing rule from PRODUCT_SPEC_001: supporting signals on the order of a handful, not a feed. Prefer one strong lead over many weak equals.

If the executive needs more, they choose depth. The Briefing does not impose encyclopaedia tax.

### How long should reading take?

For a habitual morning ritual: **minutes, not a sitting.**

A useful heuristic for product judgement (not a stopwatch metric to gamify):

- **Glance (under a minute):** know the centre  
- **Standard ritual (a few minutes):** lead + stakes + decisions understood  
- **Deep morning (optional):** one Decision/Outcome path  

If typical use requires fifteen minutes of reading to feel safe, the experience has failed Signal Over Noise — or the engine is under-suppressing (PRODUCT_SPEC_002).

### What should never overwhelm?

- Equal-weight grids  
- Unbounded insight lists  
- Charts before questions  
- Notification residue  
- Setup residue  
- Chat transcripts as home  
- Secondary surfaces leaking into the boot sequence  

Overwhelm is not “lots of enterprise data.” Overwhelm is **failure to suppress**.

### Consumption ethic

The Briefing is food for judgement, not content for engagement. Consumption principles reject infinite scroll psychology. The ethical end of the experience is the ability to close.

## 8. Interaction Philosophy

Without describing screens — how interactions should *feel*:

### Calm

Urgency is content, not chrome. Motion, if any, confirms state; it does not decorate or delay (Constitution design principles). The body of the experience should lower pulse, not raise it.

### Deliberate

Every interaction advances recognition, understanding, or a path to decide. Dead-end curiosity loops are defects. “Interesting” is not a success criterion; “decisive” is.

### Minimal

Few actions at the centre: confirm Intent when needed, open the lead judgement path, optionally deepen, exit. Minimal does not mean powerless. It means the powerful moves are obvious.

### Fast to clarity — slow to commitment

Reaching understanding is quick. Committing a decision remains deliberate and human. The experience must never rush authority while also never trapping the executive in assembly work.

### Reversible

Corrections feel light. Wrong inference is editable without shame or ceremony. Reversibility is Progressive Trust made tactile.

### Institutional, not intimate

Tone is adult and restrained. No anthropomorphism. No “I’ve been thinking about you.” Software designed around Chief of Staff *principles* — preparation, ranking, protection of attention — not Chief of Staff *cosplay*.

### Respectful of time pressure

Hit targets and flows must work when the executive has three minutes before a car. Accessibility is decision enablement (Product Principles). If it cannot be used under time pressure, it fails the experience — not merely compliance.

### Interaction anti-patterns (felt)

Reject interactions that feel like:

- **Homework** — finish setup to unlock value  
- **Theatre** — animated cleverness before clarity  
- **Negotiation** — bargain permissions for basics  
- **Surveillance warmth** — faux intimacy about “your day”  
- **Leaderboard pressure** — streaks, scores, engagement nudges  
- **Bottomless feeds** — no dignified end  

Approve interactions that feel like:

- **Prepared briefing** — reading already done  
- **Surgical deepening** — one path when chosen  
- **Clean close** — permission to stop  
- **Light correction** — truth editable  
- **Adult silence** — nothing to prove on quiet days

## 9. Empty States

Empty is not failure. Empty is sometimes truth.

### Quiet days

**Felt meaning:** Nothing requires your judgement right now. Focus Outcomes are stable enough; no binding Decision window is open; overnight change was immaterial.

**Emotional goal:** Relief without suspicion. The executive should trust silence because prior noisy days were accurate — not fear that the system is broken.

**Behaviour:** Name the quiet. Optionally note what is being watched. Do not invent filler recommendations to “add value.” Do not guilt them into exploring modules.

Quiet days teach Calm Over Noise. They are how cry-wolf is avoided.

### Busy days

**Felt meaning:** A small number of consequential claims on attention — still sparse, still ranked. Busy means *weight*, not *volume*.

**Emotional goal:** Focused seriousness. The lead story is unmistakable. Supporting items do not compete equally with the lead.

**Behaviour:** Protect the hierarchy. Resist the urge to show everything that moved. Board Mode available when they must present the same calm under social pressure. One deep path is enough; five deep paths are sabotage.

Busy days are when Confidence Through Clarity is hardest and most valuable.

### Uncertain days

**Felt meaning:** Something may matter, but grounding is thin — missing connectors, conflicting sources, stale Intent, or low confidence on impact.

**Emotional goal:** Adult partnership. Uncertainty is visible. The executive is invited to confirm, connect, or decide with eyes open — not soothed with false precision.

**Behaviour:** Label gaps. Prefer questions/drafts over prescriptions. Never fabricate pipeline, sentiment, or certainty. Offer the smallest Progressive Trust step that would improve tomorrow — after value is already partially present, not as ransom.

Uncertain days are Progressive Trust exams. Passing them builds more trust than a flashy confident day built on sand.

### Transitions between states

The experience should make state changes intelligible across mornings: yesterday quiet, today busy because X Decision matured; yesterday busy, today quieter because the Decision closed. Continuity of story is part of institutional memory — without becoming a diary the executive must manage.

## 10. Board Mode Experience

Board Mode is the same truth under presentation restraint — not a different political reality.

### What it is for

Preparing executives for leadership forums: board, ELT, investor updates, operating reviews — situations where calm authority and sparse narrative matter more than exploratory depth.

### How it should feel

| Quality | Experience |
|---------|------------|
| **Larger clarity** | The lead story and Intent can be spoken aloud without apology |
| **Less chrome** | Secondary panels recede; history and tooling noise dim |
| **Same hierarchy** | Intent → lead judgement → stakes → explained priorities |
| **No chart theatre** | Questions Before Charts remains; Board Mode is not a BI pack generator by stealth |
| **Exit is easy** | Returning to operating mode is a breath, not a reconfiguration |

Board Mode should feel like turning on a presentation posture the product already believed in — Confidence Through Restraint made explicit.

### What Board Mode must not become

- A separate analytics product  
- A place where urgency becomes visual panic for “executive impact”  
- A dump of everything “the board might ask”  
- A mode that changes recommendations to be more flattering  

The board deserves honesty as much as the morning does. Board Mode changes density and emphasis of attention — not integrity.

### Relationship to Reporting

Deeper board narratives may live in Reports (primary nav). Board Mode on the Briefing is the *feeling* of leadership-ready calm on Today’s truth — complementary, not a substitute for formal packs when those exist.

### Board Mode emotional arc

Before: mild performance anxiety, fear of missing the thread.  
During: the same centre as the morning, speakable aloud, fewer digressions.  
After: they can enter the room without a last frantic reconstruction across tools.

If Board Mode requires a separate “build the pack” project each time, it has failed the Briefing experience and drifted toward BI. The mode should reveal the Decision Operating System’s existing clarity under social conditions — not invent a new homework genre.

## 11. Success Definition

### How we know the experience succeeded

The experience succeeded when the executive achieves judgement readiness with less assembly, greater calm, and intact authority — then returns.

Concrete signals:

- They can state the lead judgement and its Outcome stakes without opening other systems  
- They know what they are deliberately not attending to today — and why that is acceptable  
- They did not need a tutorial to complete the ritual  
- They correct the system when wrong without rage-quitting  
- Quiet days feel trustworthy  
- Busy days feel focused, not flooded  
- They open Today tomorrow without being prompted by marketing mechanics  

### What executives should say after using it

Ideal language (paraphrased, not scripted copy):

- “It already knew what I’m trying to achieve.”  
- “I know what matters and what can wait.”  
- “I didn’t have to dig.”  
- “That recommendation I can argue with — here’s why I’d do something else.”  
- “Nothing needed me today — and I believe it.”  
- “I can take this into the board conversation.”  
- “I feel clearer.”  

Danger language (experience failed):

- “I still need to check Salesforce / email / the dashboard to be sure.”  
- “Everything looks urgent.”  
- “I don’t know why it’s telling me this.”  
- “It’s deciding for me.”  
- “There’s nothing here” (on a day that had real judgement needs)  
- “There’s too much here.”  
- “I’ll set this up later” (after being trapped in setup)

### Success relative to foundations

| Foundation | Experience success means |
|------------|--------------------------|
| Narrative v1.1 | First important decision without five-system reconstruction |
| PRODUCT_SPEC_001 | Hierarchy and non-goals felt, not only documented |
| PRODUCT_SPEC_002 | Sparse reasoned survivors; silence valid; explainability lived |
| Product Principles | Cognitive load down; trust up; executive decides |

## 12. Experience Principles

Every future UX decision for the Briefing — and for anything that feeds it — must follow these principles. They translate Product Principles into experiential law.

1. **Boot judgement, don’t browse modules.** Today is the OS start — not a menu of destinations.  
2. **Recognition before advice.** Mandate and stakes before prescriptions.  
3. **Sparse survivors only.** If it did not earn attention, it is invisible.  
4. **One centre of the day.** A lead claim on judgement must be unmistakable.  
5. **Explain before recommend; recommend as proposal.** Authority stays human.  
6. **Honesty over completeness.** Named gaps beat fabricated certainty.  
7. **Silence is a feature.** Quiet days are success when true.  
8. **Minutes, not sittings.** Default ritual is short; depth is chosen.  
9. **Progressive disclosure is a privilege of the user.** Never ambush with secondary worlds.  
10. **Calm is non-negotiable.** No urgency theatre; no AI spectacle.  
11. **Corrections are light.** Wrong inference is editable without ceremony.  
12. **Value before asks.** First experience earns trust before demanding commitment.  
13. **Board calm is the same truth.** Presentation restraint, not a second reality.  
14. **Chief of Staff principles, not cosplay.** Prepare judgement; do not replace leadership.  
15. **Confidence Through Clarity is the exit state.** If they leave foggy, the experience failed — regardless of feature count.

These fifteen are the experiential constitution of Today. When UX debates arise — density, tone, motion, empty states, Board Mode, AI presence — return here before inventing exceptions.
