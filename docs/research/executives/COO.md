## Purpose of the role

The COO protects **delivery truth**: capacity, quality, lead time, cost-to-serve, and operational resilience. Exceptional COOs make the organisation’s promises manufacturable (or serviceable) — and refuse silent heroics as a strategy.

---

## Primary responsibilities

- End-to-end operating system design
- Capacity, throughput, and service levels
- Quality, safety, and reliability
- Supply chain / vendor performance
- Cost-to-serve and productivity
- Incident command under disruption

---

## Mental models

| Model | Use |
|-------|-----|
| **Constraint theory (TOC)** | Elevate the binding constraint, don’t locally optimise |
| **Demonstrated capacity** | Rated capacity ≠ sustainable capacity |
| **Flow over utilisation** | High utilisation can destroy lead time |
| **Standard work + exception** | Exceptions need expiry |
| **Safety as leading system** | Culture predicts incidents |
| **OODA under disruption** | Observe–orient–decide–act faster than the failure spreads |

---

## Executive priorities

1. Promise integrity (OTIF / OTTP / SLA)  
2. Capacity honesty  
3. Resilience of critical paths  
4. Productivity without quality debt  
5. Operating cadence discipline  

---

## Decision frameworks

- Capacity envelope commit  
- Make/buy / dual-source  
- Incident severity & restart criteria  
- Overtime as exception with expiry  
- Continuous improvement with measured bottlenecks  

---

## Trade-off models

| Trade-off | Healthy COO posture |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Utilisation vs lead time | Protect flow |
| Cost vs resilience | Costed buffers on critical path |
| Speed vs quality | Quality floor non-negotiable |
| Standardisation vs local hacks | Prefer standards; time-box hacks |
| Service vs inventory | Explicit service floor |

## Questions · Signals · Indicators

**Questions:** Is this manufacturable/serviceable? What is the binding constraint? What fails if volume +10%? Are we using overtime as fake capacity?

**Signals:** Backlog, queue time, first-pass yield, supplier OTIF, incident rate, overtime %, schedule stability  

**Leading:** Constraint load, near-misses, supplier risk scores, WIP age  

**Lagging:** OTIF/OTTP, cost/unit, safety incidents, customer claims, recovery days  

---

## Rhythms · Success · Warnings

**Rhythms:** Daily ops pulse · Weekly ops review · Monthly productivity · Event disruption huddles  

**Success:** Promises kept; constraints visible; exceptions expire; improvements stick  

**Warnings:** Heroics normalised; frozen plans thrash; quality escapes with rush; one node overloaded while another idle  

---

## Disagreements · Behaviours · Board · Style

**Disagreements:** CRO (promise vs capacity); CFO (buffers vs cash); CEO (ambition vs realism); CSO (transformation vs stability); CIO (systems change vs ops risk)

**Behaviours:** Fact-based; walks the Gemba (literal or digital); calm in incidents; allergic to vanity utilisation  

**Board:** Reliability narrative; major incident truth; capacity investments justified  

**Style:** Concrete, operational, timeline-driven; prefers runbooks and owners

## Lessons · Anti-patterns · Evolution · Trends

**Lessons:** You can’t schedule away a constraint; you can only elevate or deny it.  

**Anti-patterns:** Utilisation maximisation; overtime as plan; tribal firefighting  

**Evolution:** From plant boss → enterprise operating system owner (physical + digital)  

**Trends:** Digital twins; predictive maintenance; autonomous ops; climate/physical risk

## Implications for ExecutiveOS *(synthesis)*

COO overlays must challenge unbuildable commitments and structural overtime — across every industry pack.
