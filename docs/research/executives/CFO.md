**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Canonical  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-08  
**Role code:** `cfo`

---

## Purpose of the role

The CFO protects **enterprise economic truth**: capital allocation, liquidity, margin integrity, forecast credibility, and disclosure quality. Exceptional CFOs are not scorekeepers — they are decision partners who translate strategy into cash, risk, and option value.

---

## Primary responsibilities

- Capital structure, liquidity, and funding
- Planning, forecasting, and performance management
- Investment / stage-gate discipline
- Working capital and cash conversion
- Controls, audit, and external reporting
- Partner CEO on enterprise trade-offs

---

## Mental models

| Model | Use |
|-------|-----|
| **Cash is fact, profit is opinion** | Prefer cash path under uncertainty |
| **ROIC vs growth** | Growth without returns is vanity |
| **Option value of cash** | Dry powder has strategic worth |
| **Forecast as commitment technology** | Bias and sandbagging destroy trust |
| **Unit economics first** | Scale only what works economically |
| **Disclosure as trust capital** | Ranges beat false precision |

---

## Executive priorities

1. Forecast integrity  
2. Capital allocation quality  
3. Working capital discipline

---

## Decision frameworks

- NPV / real options under uncertainty  
- Stage-gate capital (fail closed on missing evidence)  
- Zero-based challenge on structural cost  
- Scenario P&Ls (base / downside / stress)  
- “Fund the strategy, not the spreadsheet”

---

## Trade-off models

| Trade-off | Healthy CFO posture |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Growth vs cash | Explicit runway and ROIC hurdles |
| Buffer inventory vs stockouts | Costed service floor |
| Absorb cost shock vs price | Time-boxed posture |
| Capex now vs flexibility | Prefer modular unless moat-clear |
| Optimism vs conservatism | Conservatism in disclosure; clarity in ops |

---

## Questions they ask

- What is the cash path for each option?
- What return hurdle applies — and why?
- What are we deferring (write-off, liability, trust)?
- How wrong can the forecast be before this breaks?
- What will audit / board / investors challenge?

---

## Signals · Leading · Lagging

**Signals:** Cash runway, CCC, margin bridge, forecast bias, covenant headroom, concentration receivables, capex slippage  

**Leading:** Booking quality, billings vs revenue, inventory weeks cover, early warning on covenants  

**Lagging:** Free cash flow, ROIC, write-offs, audit findings, guidance misses  

---

## Executive rhythms

Weekly cash/ops finance huddle · Monthly forecast lock · Quarterly board finance · Event: M&A, crisis liquidity, material impairment  

---

## Success measures

- Forecast bias in band  
- Capital gates respected  
- CCC / liquidity in policy  
- No board finance surprises  
- Decisions costed with alternatives  

---

## Warning indicators

- Sandbagged or heroic forecasts  
- Inventory funding denial  
- Capex without benefits tracking  
- Margin “explained away” chronically  
- Working capital spikes into quarter-end cosmetics

## Executive checklists

**Investment gate:** problem · options · cash path · risks · kill criteria · owner · review date

---

## Lessons · Anti-patterns · Evolution · Trends

**Lessons:** False precision destroys trust faster than wide ranges.  

**Anti-patterns:** Spreadsheet as strategy; quarter cosmetics; veto-only CFO.  

**Evolution:** From controller → strategic capital allocator → enterprise risk partner.  

**Trends:** Real-time close; AI anomaly detection; climate/transition disclosure; working capital as strategy.

---

## Implications for ExecutiveOS *(synthesis)*

CFO overlays must always surface cash, confidence calibration, and disclosure honesty — especially under inventory, overtime, and growth scenarios.
