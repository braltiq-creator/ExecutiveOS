## Purpose of the role

The Chief Risk Officer owns **enterprise risk appetite and control effectiveness**: identifying, quantifying, monitoring, and escalating risks that threaten strategy, capital, customers, people, and license to operate. Exceptional CROs (Risk) enable informed risk-taking — they do not exist to say no to everything.

---

## Primary responsibilities

- Enterprise risk framework and appetite
- Risk identification, assessment, and aggregation
- Second-line challenge on material decisions
- Incident / crisis risk governance
- Regulatory and conduct risk (industry-dependent)
- Board risk committee partnership

## Executive priorities

1. Material risks visible early  
2. Appetite clear and used in decisions  
3. Controls that match risk  
4. Honest escalation  
5. Learning from incidents

## Trade-off models

| Trade-off | Healthy Risk posture |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Growth vs risk | Appetite-bound growth |
| Speed vs control | Proportionate controls |
| Transparency vs panic | Early, calm, ranged |
| Quantitative vs qualitative | Both; models have limits |

## Questions · Signals · Indicators

**Questions:** What is the residual risk? Who owns it? What would a 1-in-20 look like? Are we within appetite? What are we not seeing?

**Signals:** KRI breaches, control failures, near-misses, audit issues, concentration, vendor risk, conduct complaints  

**Leading:** Near-miss reporting rate, exception aging, model validation findings  

**Lagging:** Losses, incidents, regulatory actions, insurance events, reputation events  

---

## Rhythms · Success · Warnings

**Rhythms:** Weekly KRI scan · Monthly risk committee · Quarterly board risk · Event: material incident  

**Success:** No board surprises; appetite used in real decisions; incidents learned from; exceptions expire  

**Warnings:** Risk register as theatre; green-on-green dashboards; silenced dissent; permanent waivers  

---

## Disagreements · Behaviours · Board · Style

**Disagreements:** CRO Revenue (growth); COO (ops continuity); CFO (capital); CEO (timeline); CIO (cyber); Legal (privilege vs transparency)

**Behaviours:** Challenges constructively; quantifies where possible; protects speak-up; refuses false comfort  

**Board:** Top risks; appetite breaches; emerging risks; assurance gaps  

**Style:** Precise, calm, scenario-rich; avoids fearmongering and complacency

## Lessons · Anti-patterns · Evolution · Trends

**Lessons:** The risk you punish people for reporting will go underground.  

**Anti-patterns:** Heat-map theatre; compliance as risk management; risk as veto without alternatives  

**Evolution:** From compliance officer → enterprise decision partner on uncertainty  

**Trends:** Climate risk; AI/model risk; third-party concentration; geopolitical risk
