# Preface — Why This Book Exists

ExecutiveOS has completed Product Strategy, Production Readiness, Beta Planning, and Design Bible v1.0. The engineering is sound. The business model is clear. The Design Bible is competent.

**Competent is not iconic.**

Design Bible v1.0 makes safe, defensible choices:
- Executive Ink (#0F1419) — cold, developer-adjacent
- Geist Sans — contemporary but undifferentiated
- The Meridian — elegant but not memorable
- Chief of Staff archetype — service, not infrastructure
- Morning Brief layout — functional, not signature

This book exists because **safe choices do not build $10B brands.** Bloomberg did not safe-choice its terminal. Apple did not safe-choice its icon. Linear did not safe-choice its command menu.

Every chapter below deliberately adversarial. Where the Bible is right, we say so. Where a better direction exists, we recommend it with evidence.

# Studio Methodology

## The Adversarial Design Review

1. **Assume the Bible is wrong** until proven otherwise
2. **Generate quantity** before converging (10 brands, 30 logos, 12 palettes)
3. **Test against executive psychology**, not designer preference
4. **Optimize confidence-per-minute**, not time-in-app
5. **Design for the close** — last impression matters most
6. **Reject every AI cliché** — including "AI-powered executive assistant"

## Evaluation Lens

Every concept scored against:

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Calm | 20% | Does this reduce or increase executive anxiety? |
| Authority | 20% | Would a Fortune 500 board accept this? |
| Differentiation | 20% | Could a competitor copy this in a quarter? |
| Timelessness | 15% | Will this feel dated in 2036? |
| Memorability | 15% | Can you describe it to someone in one sentence? |
| Daily utility | 10% | Is this encountered every session? |

# Part 1 — Brand Positioning Exploration

*Ten completely different brand directions. None are final. Each challenges a different assumption about what ExecutiveOS is.*

---

## Direction 01 — The Navigator

**Essence:** ExecutiveOS as instrument panel for leadership — you are at the helm; the platform orients you.

| Dimension | Exploration |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Personality** | Steady, orienting, maritime calm. Speaks in bearings, not commands. |
| **Strengths** | Strong metaphor for "where am I / where am I going." Distinct from AI-assistant framing. Works globally (navigation is universal). |
| **Weaknesses** | Risk of feeling nautical or travel-adjacent. "Navigator" is crowded (HR tools, analytics). May undersell intelligence depth. |
| **Emotional response** | *"I know my heading."* Relief without urgency. |
| **Premium perception** | High if executed with aviation-instrument precision (Rolex, Bremont) — low if it reads like a GPS app. |
| **Differentiation** | Medium — Bloomberg uses "terminal" not navigator; still feels consumer-adjacent. |
| **Recommendation** | **Borrow, don't adopt.** Use navigation language in Intelligence Center copy ("bearing," "course correction") without making Navigator the master brand. |

---

## Direction 02 — The Strategist

**Essence:** ExecutiveOS as the thinking partner for long-horizon decisions — chess, not checkers.

| Dimension | Exploration |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Personality** | Reflective, analytical, patient. Values second-order thinking. |
| **Strengths** | Resonates with CEO self-image. Elevates product above task management. Natural fit for advisors and graph. |
| **Weaknesses** | Can feel slow or academic. "Strategist" implies planning, not daily operating rhythm. McKinsey owns this word culturally. |
| **Emotional response** | *"I'm playing the long game."* Intellectual satisfaction. |
| **Premium perception** | Very high in board contexts. Risk of exclusivity that alienates COOs/CFOs who want execution speed. |
| **Differentiation** | Low verbally — every B2B AI claims strategy. Must be proven through product, not naming. |
| **Recommendation** | **Use as secondary voice** for Strategy Advisor and board-facing surfaces. Not the umbrella brand. |

---

## Direction 03 — The Compass

**Essence:** True north for executive judgment — values, objectives, and priorities as magnetic field.

| Dimension | Exploration |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Personality** | Moral clarity, principled, orienting. Quieter than Navigator. |
| **Strengths** | Beautiful visual territory (single needle, cardinal restraint). Strong for values-driven CEOs. |
| **Weaknesses** | Compass is one of the most overused metaphors in SaaS (Compass CRM, etc.). Hard to trademark visually. |
| **Emotional response** | *"I'm aligned with what matters."* Grounded confidence. |
| **Premium perception** | Medium-high — depends entirely on avoiding literal compass rose clipart. |
| **Differentiation** | Low — visual and verbal collision with dozens of brands. |
| **Recommendation** | **Reject as master brand.** Design Bible was right to avoid generic navigation symbols. Extract only the "true north" narrative for onboarding. |

---

## Direction 04 — The Operating System

**Essence:** ExecutiveOS is not an app — it is the layer on which executive leadership runs.

| Dimension | Exploration |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Personality** | Foundational, inevitable, infrastructural. Speaks like kernel, not chatbot. |
| **Strengths** | **Category-defining.** Matches product strategy ("operating system for executive leadership"). Hard for Copilot to copy without admitting category loss. Creates "installed base" mental model. |
| **Weaknesses** | "OS" is overused in startup naming (every vertical claims OS). Requires product depth to justify — empty OS positioning fails publicly. |
| **Emotional response** | *"This is where I run the company from."* Ownership, permanence. |
| **Premium perception** | Highest when paired with institutional visual language — not playful tech OS. Think Bloomberg Terminal, not Windows 95. |
| **Differentiation** | **Highest of all ten directions** — no competitor owns "Executive OS" as a lived daily ritual. |
| **Recommendation** | **★ STUDIO PREFERRED DIRECTION.** Elevate from tagline to master positioning. Tagline: *"The operating system for executive leadership."* Subordinate "Chief of Staff" to feature language, not brand archetype. |

**Challenge to Design Bible:** Bible v1.0 uses "Chief of Staff" as personality archetype (Sage + Steward). Studio argues **infrastructure beats service** at scale — CEOs don't aspire to have a great assistant; they aspire to run great companies. The OS frame respects their identity.

## Direction 05 — The Decision Engine

**Essence:** ExecutiveOS converts organizational signal into decisions — throughput of judgment as the metric.

| Dimension | Exploration |

| **Premium perception** | Medium — engines are utilitarian unless paired with luxury industrial design. |
| **Differentiation** | Medium-high in EIP category; low vs. generic "decision intelligence" vendors. |
| **Recommendation** | **Adopt for product architecture narrative** (decision queue, decision velocity metrics) — not consumer-facing brand name. |

## Studio Synthesis — Recommended Brand Architecture

**Master positioning:** The Operating System  
**Master tagline:** *The operating system for executive leadership.*  
**Personality blend:** Operating System (infrastructure) + Boardroom (premium formal mode) + Decision Engine (product proof)  
**Retire as primary:** Chief of Staff archetype → demote to feature name for conversational AI  
**Visual consequence:** Move from "concierge service" warmth toward "institutional instrument" precision  

This does not reject calm — it reframes calm as **operating confidence**, not **service deference**.

# Part 6 — Intelligence Center Concepts

*Ten radically different homepage layouts. Executive psychology first. Implementation irrelevant.*

---

## Executive Psychology Primer

Before layouts: what happens in an executive's mind when they open software at 7am?

1. **Threat scan** (200ms) — Is anything on fire?
2. **Calendar anchor** (500ms) — What's my first commitment?
3. **Priority validation** (2s) — Does this match what I think matters?
4. **Delegation check** (5s) — What can I ignore or delegate?
5. **Decision mode** (10s+) — What requires my judgment?

Every layout must optimize steps 1–3. Steps 4–5 are where ExecutiveOS earns retention.

---

## Concept 01 — Mission Control

**Layout:** Full-screen dark canvas. Central health gauge (arc, 240px). Six satellite panels arranged radially — Decisions, Calendar, Risks, Initiatives, Advisors, Graph. Status bar permanent bottom.

**Psychology:** Omniscience. "I see the entire system." Appeals to control-oriented CEOs and COOs.

**Why it works:** Immediate threat scan (center gauge). Every domain visible without scroll. Bloomberg familiarity.

**Why it fails:** Violates calm promise. Dark-only alienates morning light users. Overwhelming on mobile. Surveillance energy.

**Score:** 68/100

---

## Concept 02 — Executive Briefing ★

**Layout:** FT Pink canvas (#FFF1E5). Single column, editorial. Lead story = top priority (h2, 3 sentences). Below: "Also requiring attention" (3 items, single line each). Right sidebar (desktop): calendar strip + health score compact. No cards — typography only.

**Psychology:** Morning newspaper ritual. "I've read the brief." Matches existing calendar-check habit.

**Why it works:** Fastest threat scan. Zero cognitive load from chrome. Familiar mental model (newsletter, CoS email). Mobile-perfect.

**Why it fails:** Low visual differentiation from Substack/email. May not feel "software." Hard to show advisor depth.

**Score:** 91/100 — **Studio co-favorite**

---

## Concept 03 — Control Tower

**Layout:** Top third = panoramic timeline (24-hour horizontal, events as blocks). Middle = priority queue (vertical, ranked, drag-to-reorder). Bottom = advisor strip (horizontal scroll, one insight each).

**Psychology:** Air traffic control. See the day, see the priorities, see the advisors. Sequential scanning.

**Why it works:** Calendar anchor is immediate (top). Priority queue matches mental to-do. Advisor strip adds depth without modal.

**Why it fails:** Timeline at top competes with priorities for attention. Horizontal advisor scroll is hidden affordance.

**Score:** 79/100

## Concept 04 — War Room

**Layout:** Split screen. Left 60% = active situation (single critical item, full detail, advisor reasoning expanded). Right 40% = situation board (sticky notes metaphor, categorized: Escalate / Decide / Delegate / Monitor).

**Psychology:** Crisis management. "One thing at a time." For executives in active conflict.

**Why it works:** Deep focus on highest priority. Kanban-style action sorting is intuitive. Advisor reasoning visible.

**Why it fails:** Wrong default — 90% of mornings aren't crisis. Empty war room feels broken. Too intense for calm brand.

**Score:** 62/100 — **Crisis mode only, not default homepage**

---

## Concept 05 — Decision Desk

**Layout:** Center = decision queue (3 pending decisions, card format). Each card: question, context summary, advisor recommendation, approve/defer/delegate actions inline. Health score and calendar as compact header bar. No insight cards.

**Psychology:** "My job is to decide." Positions ExecutiveOS as judgment infrastructure, not information dashboard.

**Why it works:** Aligns with Decision Register product. Action-oriented — highest retention loop. Differentiates from every competitor dashboard.

**Why it fails:** Empty decision queue = empty homepage. Ignores informational priorities that aren't formal decisions.

**Score:** 86/100 — **Strong for Decision Engine positioning**

## Concept 06 — Board Meeting

**Layout:** Table metaphor. Top = chair perspective (your name, org, date). Rows = agenda items (priority-ranked). Columns = Status / Owner / Advisor Input / Action. Gavel icon on items requiring judgment.

**Psychology:** Board meeting without the meeting. Governance, accountability, formality.

**Why it works:** Maximum enterprise signal. Natural for board-adjacent executives. Table = scannable.

**Why it fails:** Too formal for daily habit. Table doesn't work on mobile. Feels like a report, not a living system.

**Score:** 71/100 — **Board View export, not daily homepage**

# Part 7 — Signature Experience

*Every iconic product has one interaction people remember. 20+ concepts explored. One recommended.*

---

## The Signature Test

A signature interaction must be:
1. **Unique** — not copied from Linear, Notion, or Raycast
2. **Daily** — encountered in every session, not once at onboarding
3. **Executive-native** — respects judgment, not typing speed
4. **Calm** — confidence, not spectacle
5. ** Ownable** — "That's the ExecutiveOS thing"

---

## 20 Signature Interaction Concepts

### 01 — The Briefing Gesture
Swipe down anywhere to refresh intelligence. Pull reveals priority strip like pulling a briefing document from a folder. Haptic on complete.

### 02 — The Judgment Call
Three-finger tap (mobile) or `J` key (desktop) opens inline decision on any insight: Approve / Defer / Delegate. Never leaves current screen.

### 03 — The Attention Stack
Priorities are a physical stack — swipe right to dismiss (handled), swipe left to escalate (needs judgment). Stack depth visible as edge shadow.

### 04 — The Executive Pause
Hold any insight for 2 seconds → expands to full advisor reasoning without navigation. Release to collapse. Like iOS Peek but for intelligence.

### 05 — The Meridian Sweep
Horizontal scrub across timeline to replay "what changed since yesterday." Time as horizontal axis; scrubbing reveals delta.

### 06 — The Decision Stamp
When approving a decision, a subtle stamp animation (Ledger mark) appears on the item. Item moves to "Decided" archive. Satisfying, permanent.

### 07 — The Concierge Summon
Double-space anywhere (desktop) or long-press bottom bar (mobile) → Chief of Staff appears as bottom sheet. Not a chat — a briefing request.

### 08 — The Context Ring
Long-press any person, initiative, or decision → radial menu shows graph connections. "Show me everything connected to this." Knowledge graph without visiting /graph.

### 09 — The Priority Whisper
First item in priority queue auto-reads aloud on launch (opt-in). 15-second audio brief. Like a CoS speaking quietly. Accessibility + executive car commute.

### 10 — The Intelligence Pulse
Health score dot in status bar pulses once when score changes >5 points since last visit. Subtle. No notification — ambient awareness.

### 11 — The Advisor Circle
⌘+1 through ⌘+0 summon specific advisors as floating panels. Each advisor has a position on an invisible decagon. Spatial memory for power users.

### 12 — The Delegation Handoff
Drag any priority item to a team member avatar → creates delegated task with context attached. Physical metaphor for "handing off."

### 13 — The Board Preview
Pinch-to-zoom any insight card → expands to full board-slide format. Ready to AirPlay. ExecutiveOS as presentation tool.

### 14 — The Memory Anchor
Highlight any text in any advisor conversation → "Remember this" → saved to Executive Memory with source link. One gesture, permanent institutional memory.

### 15 — The Silence Mode
Toggle in status bar: suppresses all non-critical updates. UI dims 10%. ExecutiveOS respects "I'm in a board meeting."

### 16 — The Morning Threshold
App won't show full dashboard until user completes 3-second "read the lead story" pause. Ensures top priority is seen. Controversial but effective.

### 17 — The Operating Slash
Type `/` anywhere → not a command palette but a **judgment palette**: "What should I decide today?" / "What's at risk?" / "Prepare me for [meeting]." Executive-native commands.

### 18 — The Twin Mirror
Split-screen mode: left = what ExecutiveOS knows about you; right = what you said your priorities are. Divergence highlighted. Digital twin concept made visual.

### 19 — The Ledger Scroll
Scroll up past top of Intelligence Center → reveals "Yesterday's Record" — archived brief from previous day. Pull-down becomes pull-up. Historical consciousness.

### 20 — The Confidence Exit
When closing app, single line: "3 decisions made today. 2 initiatives on track. Nothing critical overnight." Exit summary. Last emotion = confidence.

### 21 — The Attention Budget
Status bar shows "Attention used: 4/7 items" — explicit cognitive load metric. When budget exceeded, ExecutiveOS suggests deferrals. Unique, executive-native.

### 22 — The Circadian Shift
Background and layout automatically transition Morning Brief → Operating mode at 10am with 400ms crossfade. User feels the product knows their rhythm.

## ★ THE SIGNATURE: #20 — The Confidence Exit

**What it is:** When an executive closes ExecutiveOS (app switch, tab close, logout), a single calm line appears for 2 seconds:

> *"3 decisions made today. 2 initiatives on track. Nothing critical overnight."*

Dynamic content. Always ends with confidence. Never anxiety.

**Why this is the one they'll remember in 10 years:**

1. **Last impression = lasting impression.** Every session ends with calm, not cliffhanger.
2. **No competitor does this.** Slack says "You have unread messages." Bloomberg says nothing. ExecutiveOS says "You're in control."
3. **It's the brand promise delivered.** "You will always know what requires your attention" — inverted as "you've handled what required your attention."
4. **It's executive-native.** Counts decisions and initiatives — not messages, not tasks, not emails.
5. **It creates withdrawal comfort.** Executives close the app feeling finished, not FOMO. They return tomorrow because leaving felt good, not because anxiety pulled them back (Slack model).
6. **It's shareable.** "ExecutiveOS always tells me I'm done." Word of mouth for a product category that has none.

**Runner-up signature (implement both):** #21 Attention Budget — visible in status bar daily. #06 Decision Stamp — moment of judgment permanence.

**Challenge to Design Bible:** Bible v1.0 defines no signature interaction. This is the single largest gap between "good product" and "iconic product."

# Part 9 — Competitor Experience Analysis

*What ExecutiveOS should borrow from ten products — and what it must reject.*

## Apple

**What they do brilliantly:**
- **Progressive disclosure** — complexity hidden until needed (Settings vs. System Preferences)
- **Confidence through whitespace** — product pages breathe; nothing competes
- **Motion with purpose** — every animation orients (sheet slide = "this is temporary")
- **Last-session continuity** — devices remember where you were
- **Premium unboxing** — first moment sets lifetime perception

**Borrow for ExecutiveOS:**
- Whitespace discipline in Intelligence Center morning mode
- Sheet-based advisor panels (temporary, dismissible)
- "Continuity" — Intelligence Center remembers scroll position and expanded sections

**Reject:**
- Consumer playfulness (emoji reactions, Memoji)
- Ecosystem lock-in assumptions
- Feature discovery through exploration (executives want direct paths)

---

## Linear

**What they do brilliantly:**
- **Keyboard-first power** — ⌘K command palette is muscle memory
- **Speed as feature** — instant transitions, optimistic UI, no loading states
- **Status as design** — workflow states encoded in minimal visual language
- **Issue = unit of work** — everything is an actionable item
- **Restrained color** — purple accent on neutral; color means something

**Borrow for ExecutiveOS:**
- Command palette architecture (adapt to judgment commands, not dev commands)
- Optimistic UI for decision actions (stamp immediately, sync async)
- Status encoding: on-track / at-risk / off-track as minimal dot + label

**Reject:**
- Developer-centric vocabulary (issues, cycles, sprints)
- Dark-mode-only aesthetic
- Density that assumes 8-hour screen time (executives use 30 minutes)

## Notion

**What they do brilliantly:**
- **Slash command** — `/` as universal creation interface
- **Block model** — everything is composable
- **Empty canvas invitation** — "Start here" without prescribing structure
- **Template gallery** — reduce blank-page anxiety
- **Database views** — same data, multiple perspectives

**Borrow for ExecutiveOS:**
- `/` as Operating Slash (judgment commands)
- Multiple views on same data (Decision Register as table / timeline / graph)
- Template briefs for board prep, quarterly review

**Reject:**
- Blank canvas as default (executives want pre-assembled intelligence)
- Customization burden (executives won't build their own dashboards)
- Wiki aesthetic (ExecutiveOS is curated, not authored)

**Borrow for ExecutiveOS:**

- Orange-on-black aesthetic

- Training requirement (Bloomberg requires certification; ExecutiveOS must not)

## Appendix G — Extended Brand Direction Narratives

### The Operating System — Extended Narrative

When Marc Benioff called Salesforce "the end of software," he was wrong about the end but right about the ambition. ExecutiveOS can credibly claim **"the beginning of executive infrastructure"** — because unlike CRM (departmental) or ERP (operational), executive intelligence is genuinely unoccupied at the platform level.

The OS metaphor works because:
1. **Installations compound** — every decision, memory, and connection makes switching costly
2. **Other apps orbit it** — M365, Salesforce, calendar — ExecutiveOS integrates, doesn't replace
3. **Daily boot sequence** — morning brief is literally "booting up" the executive's day
4. **System preferences** — executive profile, advisor configuration, attention budget = OS settings
5. **Kernel vs. apps** — Intelligence Center is kernel; Advisors, Graph, Calendar are apps

The risk of OS metaphor: dated Windows nostalgia. Mitigation: **instrument aesthetic** (Braun, not Microsoft). ExecutiveOS is a precision instrument, not a computer.

### The Ledger Line — Extended Narrative

The ledger is the oldest executive technology. Before dashboards, before spreadsheets, before computers — leaders kept ledgers. Double-entry bookkeeping created modern commerce. The executive who keeps the ledger knows the truth.

ExecutiveOS is the digital ledger of executive judgment:
- Every decision recorded (Decision Register)
- Every priority noted (Intelligence Center)
- Every advisor consultation logged (Advisor panels)
- Every memory preserved (Executive Memory)

The Ledger Line mark — two parallel rules — is the ruling on ledger paper. It says: *everything is recorded, nothing is lost, the books balance.*

This is stronger than Meridian (navigation) because it connects to **product truth** (decision recording) not just **metaphor** (timelines).

## Appendix H — Circadian Design Research Notes

### Why circadian design is defensible

1. **Chronobiology literature** supports cognitive peak variation across day (Schmidt et al., 2007)
2. **Executive morning routines** are consistent: threat scan → calendar → priorities (Harvard Business Review, "How CEOs Manage Time")
3. **Warm light exposure** improves reading comprehension vs. cold light (Kruithof curve)
4. **Dark mode adoption** peaks 6pm–6am in SaaS analytics (industry data)
5. **No competitor** implements time-aware UI — genuine white space

### Circadian design risks

1. **Inconsistency perception** — "the app looks different" — mitigated by intentional, explained transition
2. **Timezone complexity** — travel executives — mitigated by manual override + location detection
3. **Engineering cost** — token switching — mitigated by CSS custom property architecture already in place
4. **Designer complexity** — three modes to maintain — mitigated by shared component library, different tokens only

### Recommendation: Ship Briefing mode first (FT Pink morning). Circadian auto-switch in v1.0.

## Appendix I — Signature Interaction Detailed Specs

### Confidence Exit — Full Specification

**Trigger conditions:**
- Tab close (beforeunload)
- App background (mobile)
- Explicit logout
- Session idle >30 minutes (on return, show as welcome back variant)

**Copy templates:**
```
Standard: "{n} decisions made today. {n} initiatives on track. Nothing critical overnight."
Variant (no decisions): "Quiet day. {n} initiatives on track. Nothing requires your attention."
Variant (critical pending): "{n} decisions made today. 1 item may require attention tomorrow morning."
Welcome back: "Since yesterday: {n} changes. {lead priority headline}."
```

**Rules:**
- Never exceed 2 sentences
- Never use exclamation marks
- Never use red/orange text
- Always end calm
- Critical pending items surfaced gently — "may require attention," not "URGENT"

**Animation:** Fade in 200ms. Hold 2 seconds. Fade out 200ms. No slide. No bounce.

**Measurement:** Track `confidence_exit_viewed` event. Target: ≥80% of sessions.

### Attention Budget — Full Specification

**Calculation:**
- Each priority item requiring executive judgment (not delegation) = 1 attention unit
- Default budget: 7 units/day (Miller's Law adjacency — 7±2)
- CoS-configurable: 5–12 range

**Display:** `Attention: 4/7` in status bar. Muted text. No red until 7/7.

**Overflow behavior:** When budget exceeded, ExecutiveOS suggests: "3 items remain. Recommend deferring 2 to tomorrow. [Review] [Dismiss]"

**Measurement:** Track attention budget overflow events. Target: <20% of days exceed budget.

### Decision Stamp — Full Specification

**Trigger:** User approves decision via Judgment Call or Decision Desk.

**Animation:**
1. Ledger mark appears centered on decision card (0ms)
2. Scale 0.8→1.0 (100ms, ease-out)
3. Opacity 0→1 (100ms)
4. Hold 400ms
5. Card slides to archive with stamp visible (200ms)
6. Total: 800ms

**Sound:** Optional subtle stamp sound (paper thud). Off by default. Accessibility: never sound-only.

## E.4 — Colour Psychology Deep Dive

### Why navy beats black for ExecutiveOS

Black (#000000) signals: luxury fashion (Chanel), developer tools (Linear), death/formality (varies by culture).  
Navy (#1A1F2E) signals: institution (McKinsey, Goldman), trust (banking), permanence (universities), authority without severity.

ExecutiveOS is not a fashion brand or developer tool. Navy is correct institutional signal.

### Why FT Pink works for morning

#FFF1E5 (salmon pink) signals: Financial Times (editorial authority), morning (warm light), print (paper), calm (not alert red/yellow).  
It is the only colour in the 12-palette exploration that triggers **reading behavior** rather than **software behavior**.

Morning brief is a reading experience, not a dashboard experience. The colour should trigger reading.

### Why critical red must be muted (#9B2226 not #DC2626)

Bright red (#DC2626) triggers amygdala response — fight/flight. Executives in fight/flight make poor decisions.  
Muted red (#9B2226) signals importance without panic. Bloomberg uses orange for similar reason — attention without alarm.

## E.8 — Notification Philosophy (Extended)

### The Notification Hierarchy

| Tier | Channel | Frequency cap | Example |
|------|---------|---------------|---------|
| Critical | Push + in-app | 1/day max | "Initiative Aurora off-track. Board exposure." |
| Important | In-app only | 3/day max | "Decision pending >48 hours." |
| Informational | Digest only | Unlimited in digest | "Strategy advisor updated recommendation." |
| Silent | None | — | Health score changed 2 points |

### The Notification Test

Before sending any notification, ask:
1. Would a excellent Chief of Staff interrupt the CEO with this? If no → don't send.
2. Can this wait until morning brief? If yes → defer.
3. Is there a specific action required? If no → digest only.
4. Is this critical to today's board meeting? If yes → send.

**Target metric:** 80% of executive-days with zero push notifications.
