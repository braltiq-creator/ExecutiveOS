**Version:** 1.0.0

---

## Purpose of the role

The CEO holds **enterprise coherence**: integrating strategy, capital, operations, customers, people, and risk into a single judgement surface. Exceptional CEOs do not run every function — they decide which trade-offs the organisation will live with, and own the narrative to the board and organisation.

---

## Primary responsibilities

- Set and protect Focus Outcomes (few, material, owned)
- Allocate scarce attention, capital, and capacity
- Arbitrate cross-functional conflict
- Own board trust and external credibility
- Hire, develop, and occasionally remove senior leaders
- Establish decision rights and operating cadence

---

## Mental models

| Model | How exceptional CEOs use it |
|-------|----------------------------|
| **Portfolio of outcomes** | Enterprise as a few Focus Outcomes, not a KPI zoo |
| **Attention as capital** | Leadership time is scarcer than money |
| **Option value** | Preserve strategic optionality under uncertainty |
| **Coherence test** | “If this wins locally, does the enterprise still win?” |
| **Trust compounding** | Board and organisation trust compounds or decays with narrative honesty |
| **Cynefin / complexity** | Distinguish clear, complicated, complex, chaotic domains before choosing process |

---

## Executive priorities

1. Organisation Health and strategic clarity  
2. Decision quality under time pressure  
3. Capital and capacity alignment to strategy  
4. Leadership bench and culture  
5. Board-ready truth  

---

## Decision frameworks

- **Outcome-first decision:** Which Focus Outcome moves? By how much? At what cost to others?
- **One-way vs two-way doors** (Bezos): speed vs irreversibility
- **Pre-mortem:** “It is 12 months later and this failed — why?”
- **Disagree and commit:** Surface dissent, then execute
- **CEO as integrator:** Prefer options that restore system coherence

---

## Trade-off models

| Trade-off | CEO bias when healthy |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Growth vs margin | Explicit time-boxed posture |
| Speed vs certainty | Bias to speed on two-way doors |
| Local optimisation vs enterprise | Enterprise wins |
| Optimism vs honesty | Honesty to board; optimism in mobilisation |
| Transformation vs reliability | Stage-gated; never silent reliability debt |

---

## Questions they ask

- What is the real decision — and what is noise?
- Which Focus Outcome is at stake?
- Who loses if we choose this?
- What would make us wrong?
- What will the board ask in 30/90 days?
- Are we confusing motion with progress?

---

## Signals they monitor

- Outcome portfolio drift  
- Leadership capacity / attention contention  
- Cross-functional escalation volume  
- Customer concentration or strategic logo risk  
- Culture and key-person risk  
- External narrative (media, investors, regulators)

---

## Leading indicators

- Decision cycle time on material items  
- Agenda quality (outcomes vs updates)  
- Early escalation of bad news  
- Forecast honesty vs sandbagging  

## Lagging indicators

- Outcome attainment  
- ROIC / enterprise value proxies  
- Voluntary attrition of high performers  
- Board surprise incidents  

---

## Executive rhythms

| Cadence | Ritual |
|---------|--------|
| Daily | Personal Command Centre / priority scan |
| Weekly | ELT — trade-offs, not theatre |
| Monthly | Operating / strategy review |
| Quarterly | Board |
| Event | Crisis / material risk huddle |

---

## Success measures

- Focus Outcomes move with explained variance  
- Material decisions are timely and owned  
- Board receives ranges, not surprises  
- ELT disagrees in the room, aligns outside  
- Organisation can state priorities without a slide  

---

## Warning indicators

- ELT meetings become status broadcasts  
- Chronic firefighting displaces strategy  
- Optimistic board language vs operational reality  
- Key decisions deferred past cost-of-delay threshold  
- Heroics required to hit plan  

---

## Typical disagreements with other executives

| With | Conflict pattern |
|------|------------------|
| CFO | Growth ambition vs capital discipline |
| COO | Promise to market vs capacity truth |
| CRO | Revenue optimism vs enterprise risk |
| CSO | Long-term bets vs near-term delivery |
| CPO | Performance honesty vs retention politics |
| Risk/Legal | Speed vs compliance |

---

## Leadership behaviours

- Asks clarifying questions before opinions  
- Makes trade-offs explicit  
- Credits dissent that proves correct  
- Protects truth-tellers  
- Removes ambiguity about owners  

---

## Board expectations

- Coherent strategy and capital story  
- Early escalation of material risk  
- Credible management bench  
- Honest ranges on outlook  
- Clear ask when deciding  

---

## Communication style

Sparse, outcome-linked, decision-oriented. Prefers one page + options over decks. Separates facts, judgements, and asks.

## Executive checklists

**Before a material decision**

- [ ] Focus Outcome named  
- [ ] Options ≥2  
- [ ] Dissent heard  
- [ ] Cost of delay stated  
- [ ] Review trigger set  

**Weekly ELT**

- [ ] Trade-offs on agenda  
- [ ] Owners named  
- [ ] No undecided items left ambiguous  

---

## Lessons learned

- Clarity beats charisma under pressure  
- The organisation copies what the CEO tolerates  
- Deferred decisions are still decisions  

---

## Decision anti-patterns

- Consensus as avoidance  
- “More data” as delay on irreversible windows  
- Local heroics celebrated over system fixes  
- Strategy as slogan without resource commitment  

---

## Evolution of the role

From “chief administrator” → “chief capital allocator” → “chief judgement officer” under information overload. AI increases the premium on framing and trust, not on raw information access.

---

## Future trends

- AI-augmented briefings; human-owned judgement  
- Stakeholder capitalism pressures on disclosure  
- Geopolitical and supply resilience as CEO issues  
- Attention scarcity as explicit management object  

---

## Implications for ExecutiveOS *(synthesis)*

CEO is the integrator of Focus Outcomes, Council arbitration, and board narrative. Packs must never bypass CEO coherence with functional dashboards.
