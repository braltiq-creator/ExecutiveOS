## Purpose of the role

The CPO (People) owns **human capital as a strategic system**: leadership quality, organisation design, culture, capability, and workforce risk. Exceptional CPOs make people decisions economic and cultural — not administrative.

---

## Primary responsibilities

- Leadership succession and performance
- Organisation design and decision rights
- Talent acquisition for critical capabilities
- Culture, engagement, and conduct
- Total rewards aligned to strategy
- Workforce planning and change leadership

---

| Model | Use |

## Questions · Signals · Indicators

**Questions:** Who are the 20 people we cannot afford to lose? Is the org design creating the politics we see? Are we overloading change? Do managers avoid hard conversations?

**Signals:** eNPS, regrettable attrition, time-to-fill critical roles, span of control, manager quality scores, conduct cases  

**Leading:** Offer accept rate, internal mobility, succession coverage, manager 1:1 cadence  

**Lagging:** Voluntary attrition, regrettable loss, engagement, diversity of leadership slate, productivity proxies  

---

## Rhythms · Success · Warnings

**Rhythms:** Weekly talent risk · Monthly calibration · Quarterly org/succession · Event: reorg / crisis people  

**Success:** Critical roles covered; performance differentiated; culture matches stated values; change succeeds  

**Warnings:** High performer exodus; zombie roles; reorg churn; survey theatre; tolerance of brilliant jerks

## Lessons · Anti-patterns · Evolution · Trends

**Lessons:** Strategy fails at the first under-filled critical role.  

**Anti-patterns:** Engagement as goal; reorg as strategy; HR as policy police only  

**Evolution:** From admin HR → chief organisation effectiveness officer  

**Trends:** Skills-based orgs; AI in talent; hybrid work design; wellbeing as productivity factor
